World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026: Russia and Ukraine agreed the war's largest prisoner exchange — more than 1,000 on each side, Russian officials said — as two days of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi ended with no ceasefire and no deal on territory; envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff travel to Moscow to meet Vladimir Putin on Thursday (Reuters, Kyiv Independent). At COP31 in Antalya, Turkey, UN chief Antonio Guterres told 102 world leaders the planet was on a "path to mutually assured destruction" and demanded $300bn a year in climate finance by 2035 toward a $1.3tn goal, while the US skipped the talks for a second year (Reuters). US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said economic pressure had made major combat operations against Iran unnecessary, while Tehran's negotiator said the Strait of Hormuz, shut for over 250 days, would "never return" to its pre-war state (New York Times, Al Jazeera). Israeli strikes killed at least seven people in Gaza, including a father and his 13-year-old son, as the year-old truces in Gaza and Lebanon frayed, Palestinian health officials said. The Democratic Republic of Congo reported 11,042 Ebola cases and 5,387 deaths, its worst outbreak in years. In Washington, House Republicans blocked an effort to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, 211-204, and elected Jim Jordan of Ohio as their new leader, 106-73. US prosecutors charged three Iran-backed operatives over alleged plots in the US and Europe, and British police arrested a US Army officer on suspicion of spying for Russia. Israel's new parliament was sworn in after a deadlocked election left Benjamin Netanyahu without a majority and no clear path to a government. NATO scrambled jets after drones strayed into Baltic airspace during Ukrainian strikes on Russia. In Sudan, the army said it shot down drones as fighting spread in West Kordofan and the power grid came under attack. Nvidia shares fell 5.4% to $169.04, nearly 10% down over two sessions.
